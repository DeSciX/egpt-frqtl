# The resolution gate — structure below the grain does not exist

> How-To-See corpus. Method only; the numbers live in your recipe.
> Source: FRAQTL visual-bite skill §2.2 @ `335bbc15`; distilled 2026-08-07.

A pixel is the smallest unit of space; a tick the smallest unit of time. Structure has
a fixed size **in units of λ**, so shrinking λ shrinks the structure in pixels until it
falls through the grid and **stops existing** — not a weaker pattern, NO pattern.

## The check (before you build, no run needed)

Your recipe states the structure size as a fraction of λ, and the λ that clears the
grain on its stage. Multiply:

```
structure_px = fraction × λ
```

Below a few pixels you will see nothing, and **no amount of running fixes it** — this
is not a counting problem (see `two-clocks.md`); it is geometry.

## Choosing λ

**Choose λ as large as the stage allows**, then confirm every predicted feature lands
inside the canvas with margin. If they don't all fit, claim FEWER features — **one
well-resolved pair of bands beats three mushy ones** — and say so out loud in your
prediction. Do not change the stage size your recipe names; the stage is shared ground
that makes results comparable.

## The tell

A pattern that "reads as a smooth hump" where bands were predicted is the classic
below-grain signature. Before concluding anything about the physics, check whether
`structure_px` at your λ is under a few pixels. If it is, the experiment was invisible
by construction — raise λ or claim less; never explain the hump with new physics.
