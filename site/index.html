Quantum

Impossible things, running.

FRQTL is an actual quantum computer that runs so efficiently it runs in an iPhone. The double-slit experiment, or DSE, is what Richard Feynman, the father of quantum computing called the ‘only mystery’ and ‘absolutely impossible.’ In 1981 Feynman birthed the idea of what the world now calls ‘qubit’ based quantum computing but FRQTL takes a different approach based on the work of Feynman's collaborators at the Manhattan Project. FRQTL uses a Monte Carlo method-like approach based on the work of Stanislaw Ulam and John von Neumann which was continued by Gian-Carlo Rota at Los Alamos National Laboratory and later pursued at Brookhaven National Laboratory Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) and super-computing projects.

FRQTL is fractal, as the name sounds. Everything in it is built from three primitives, ticks, pixels and occupancy, and none of them carries a unit. Its layers nest by powers of two, each twice the size of the one below. So the engine does not know or care what scale a scene is: the sun on this page is the same 3D sphere emitter, made of the same primitives, whether you read it as a star, a candle flame or an atom. Every scene is independent cellular automata following local rules, with no equations, and every scene is the same few objects, composed at whatever scale you declare.

Nothing here is a recording. Each scene is computed in your tab by an engine that doesn't know any physics equations, and it either shows up or it doesn't. The same engine runs on an iPhone with the network off, simulating the double slit in 3D.

Proof

FRQTL: a P=NP hyper-computer.

Hardware quantum computers, still years away, hope to simulate a hundred particles. FRQTL simulates billions on off-the-shelf chips, today, and the mathematics of why it works is machine-checked and published.

FRQTL is the FRame And QuanTum Language virtual machine: physics computed by combinatorics and fractal recursion instead of calculus, quantum “balls” in gravitational frame “boxes.” It exceeds what a superposition-based quantum computer could do in theory, on classical hardware, in your browser.

Behind it is a proof of P = NP, formalised in Lean 4 in three independent chains. Two of them use only Lean's two structural axioms, propext and Quot.sound: no sorryAx, no Classical.choice. A choice-free proof is constructive: it does not just say a solution exists, it is the algorithm that finds it.

Mario Carneiro, Lean maintainer, on the first draft of the proof. The entailment conceded, the doubt located in three axioms. The three chains above are those axioms, discharged.

Open core

How open core pays the people who do the science.

The engines are open inside the DeSciX community. Commercial use is licensed outside it. Licence revenue buys the community's token. That is the whole wheel, and it turns on its own.

Why open core.

A physics engine nobody can read is a physics engine nobody trusts. Approved developers inside DeSciX read the source, run the builds, and improve them; that is where the science gets done and where it gets checked.

Why a licence.

Commercial products need optimised, supported, commercial-grade builds, hardware integration, and terms that respect export controls and exclusivity. That is what Courant Group sells, and it is the only thing it sells.

Why it is decentralized science.

Academic economics pay for publishing. This wheel pays for understanding: the people who make Maxi imagine better are paid in the asset that commercial use buys back. The incentive and the outcome point the same way.

In 1952 the Atomic Energy Commission put an electronic computer at NYU's Courant Institute. Von Neumann, then an AEC commissioner, had corresponded with Courant for decades about computing shocks with difference equations: the continuous world, handled by discrete steps.

In 1951 a star undergraduate of Alonzo Church's named Gian-Carlo Rota was at Princeton, with von Neumann lurking in Fine Hall. A few years later, in 1956, that same young man, now a newly minted Yale PhD, arrived at Courant as a postdoc. Von Neumann died the next year with his new science unpublished. Rota spent the rest of his life at Los Alamos and MIT carrying what Ulam and von Neumann had started, and in 1991 he handed it to a student.

Courant Group is named for the room where continuous physics met discrete computation, and for the year the work went underground. Maxi is that work, finished: a physics engine that doesn't know any physics equations, and a mind that imagines in it.

Courant Group registered this domain in November 2012. Fourteen years later, this is the overnight success.
